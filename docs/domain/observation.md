# Domain Card: Observation

Let's imagine we are writing the dictionary of the QTLab universe.

---

## Definition

>An Observation is the recognition of a potentially interesting market phenomenon that motivates further investigation.

Notice what it doesn't say.

*It doesn't say "pattern."*

*It doesn't say "signal."*

*It doesn't say "strategy."*

An observation is simply something that made a researcher curious.

---

## Why does it exist?

Without observations there would be no research.

Every research project begins because someone noticed something.

Examples:

>"Gold tends to rise when real interest rates fall."

>"The VIX spikes before major corrections."

>"Large ETFs often recover after unusually large daily declines."

These are not claims.

They are invitations to investigate.

---

## What problem does it solve?

Researchers constantly have ideas.

The problem is that most ideas disappear.

An Observation allows researchers to capture curiosity before it is forgotten.

Think of it as a research notebook entry.

---

## Who creates it?

A Researcher.

Not QTLab.

Not AI.

Not a methodology.

Humans observe.

QTLab records.

---

## What can it become?

An Observation can evolve into one or more Research Questions.

Example:

Observation:

>"Silver seems to recover after panic selling."

Possible research questions:

- Do silver ETFs exhibit statistically significant mean reversion?
- Does recovery depend on volatility?
- Is the effect stronger during bull markets?
- Does it disappear after transaction costs?

One observation can inspire many questions.

---

## What does it own?

Only information that belongs to the observation itself.

For example:

- Title
- Description
- Date recorded
- Researcher
- Source (optional)
- Tags
- Confidence (optional)
- Notes

Notice what's missing.

No datasets.

No trades.

No results.

No parameters.

---

## What does it explicitly NOT own?

This is just as important.

An Observation does not contain:

- hypotheses
- experiments
- conclusions
- statistics
- charts
- trading rules

Otherwise it starts becoming something else.

---

## Lifecycle

Observations should NOT have many states.

Maybe only:

    Captured
         │
         ▼
    Under Investigation
         │
         ├─────────────► Archived
         │
         ▼
    Explored

Simple.

---

## Relationships
    Observation
    ↓
    Research Question
    ↓
    Hypothesis

Notice that the Observation remains unchanged.

The research evolves around it.

---

## Examples
### Good Observation

>"European utility stocks appear resilient during periods of rising interest rates."

Good.

Interesting.

Investigable.


### Bad Observation

>"Buy utilities whenever rates rise."

That's already a strategy.

Too late.

### Another Bad Observation

>"Utilities outperform because investors always seek dividends."

That's already an explanation.

It belongs in a hypothesis.
